## Preface

I was unfamiliar with Brother Lawrence until I began exploring the contemplative traditions of Christianity. As I encountered writers who spoke about silence, attention, and the possibility of a life rooted more deeply in God, his name began to appear. What drew me to him was the simplicity of his approach. He seemed to offer a way of prayer that could accompany the ordinary life I was already living.

Much of our experience encourages us to believe that meaningful change requires something more: greater knowledge, a better method, stronger discipline, or circumstances that finally give us room to begin. We can bring these same expectations into our spiritual lives. We imagine that closeness to God belongs to a future version of ourselves, someone less distracted and more capable of sustained devotion. Brother Lawrence invites us to turn toward God within our present circumstances, bringing our unfinished lives into the relationship.

A seventeenth-century Carmelite lay brother, Brother Lawrence carried out ordinary responsibilities within a religious community. He became especially associated with the monastery kitchen, where repetitive tasks and competing demands gave him daily opportunities to practice what he taught. His wisdom reaches us through recorded conversations and letters, in which he repeatedly returns to a familiar invitation: remember God, speak simply, act with love, and return gently when attention wanders.

There is something deeply welcoming about a spirituality that can be practiced while preparing a meal or tending to someone’s needs. Most of us spend our days among responsibilities that rarely feel remarkable. We answer messages, clean rooms, travel to work, care for our bodies, and begin again with tasks we completed yesterday. Brother Lawrence helps us consider how these activities might become places of communion, receiving spiritual depth through the love and attention we bring to them.

This is what I find most compelling in his teaching: the possibility that our relationship with God can become woven into the whole of life. We can share a moment of gratitude, acknowledge irritation before it becomes a careless word, ask for help when we feel overwhelmed, or offer a small task for someone else’s good. These movements are modest enough to be overlooked. Yet through them, prayer can become increasingly familiar, shaping how we inhabit the hours we have been given.

Brother Lawrence’s simplicity should not be mistaken for an absence of struggle. He describes years of inward anguish, persistent distraction, and repeated failure. The peace associated with his name developed within a life that included uncertainty and considerable effort. His account offers encouragement precisely because he knew how difficult it could be to live according to a sincere intention.

One of his most helpful teachings concerns what happens when we forget. He encourages a peaceful return, recognizing that agitation over distraction can scatter the mind still further. We acknowledge where we have wandered and turn toward God again. Over time, this repeated return can nurture a relationship less dominated by anxious self-examination and more open to honesty, gratitude, and trust.

This book brings together contemporary retellings of Brother Lawrence’s four conversations and fifteen letters, alongside sixty daily devotional readings. The retellings aim to preserve his meaning, images, historical details, and spiritual concerns in accessible language. The reflections then explore how his teaching might meet the circumstances of a contemporary reader. His words provide the starting point; the devotionals offer a conversation with them.

Three introductory readings open the journey. Each conversation and letter is then followed by three devotionals, allowing time to approach its themes from different angles. Each daily reading includes a reflection, a practical invitation, and a brief breath prayer. Together, these elements offer ways to carry the reading into work, relationships, rest, and the quieter intervals of the day.

The practices are intended to be approachable. You may find that a particular invitation fits naturally into your circumstances, while another needs adjustment. The breath prayers provide a few words to accompany an easy, unforced breath, helping the heart remember what the mind has considered. You are free to linger over a reading, revisit an earlier entry, or move more slowly than the sixty-day structure suggests.

Readers will also encounter teachings that feel unfamiliar or difficult. Brother Lawrence writes from within his own religious world, using severe language about human sinfulness and sometimes interpreting illness as something God sends for spiritual healing. In certain letters, his counsel about medical remedies reflects judgments we should not adopt as present-day guidance. Faithful attention to a historical writer includes recognizing these limits, alongside receiving the insight and tenderness his work offers.

The retellings preserve these tensions so that we can meet him honestly. The accompanying reflections make room for questions, grief, discernment, and the practical care that suffering requires. Trust in God can accompany treatment, appropriate boundaries, and the support of other people. We can seek communion amid difficulty while continuing to ask for relief and work toward healing.

Brother Lawrence also describes experiences of joy and inward assurance that may seem far removed from our own. These accounts need not become standards against which we assess our faith. His invitations remain available on days when prayer feels quiet, distracted, or emotionally uneventful. We can offer the attention we have and allow the relationship to deepen without demanding a particular experience.

My hope is that these pages will help us become more attentive to the sacred depth of ordinary life. Perhaps we will begin to recognize opportunities for love within tasks we once hurried through, or become more willing to bring our actual feelings into prayer. Perhaps we will learn to receive care with greater trust and respond to our own imperfections with the humility that makes another beginning possible.

As you begin, bring the life that is already yours: its responsibilities and pleasures, its unanswered questions, and whatever longing has drawn you to these pages. There is room to speak honestly, to listen, and to return as often as you need. Brother Lawrence’s invitation can begin in the next ordinary moment, as we turn toward the God whose presence we are learning to recognize.
