## Epilogue — Into the Life Before You

Soon you will close this book and turn toward something else. There may be a meal to prepare, a message awaiting your reply, or someone nearby who would welcome your attention. Perhaps the house is quiet, and a few unclaimed minutes remain before the next responsibility. These are the circumstances in which the invitation of these pages can continue to unfold.

Brother Lawrence has accompanied us through kitchens and conversations, seasons of inward struggle, and letters exchanged with people carrying their own concerns. Again and again, he has directed our attention toward a relationship available within ordinary life. His counsel can travel with us as we leave the readings behind, becoming familiar through the small occasions on which we remember to respond.

You might pause before reaching for your phone and notice the morning light. While washing a cup, you might give thanks for the person who shared a drink with you. An unexpected difficulty may draw forth a simple request for help, while a moment of delight becomes something you want to share with God. Each offers its own beginning, brief enough to fit within the day and honest enough to belong to your life.

There will be many hours when you forget. You may finish an entire day before remembering the intention with which you began it, or discover that irritation has overtaken the patience you hoped to offer. When you notice, allow yourself to return. If something needs repair, attend to it; if you are tired, receive the rest or assistance available to you. Your next prayer can begin with the truth of where you are.

Over time, remembrance may become less separate from the people and places before you. Turning toward God may help you hear the weariness beneath someone’s words, recognize the care your body needs, or receive a kindness you would otherwise dismiss. The relationship deepens as it enters the particulars of your life. Attention becomes an opportunity for love to take a visible form.

Some moments will ask very little of you. You may sit beside a window, feel the warmth of a familiar hand, or listen to rain without searching for anything to say. Let there be room for this quiet enjoyment. A shared life includes the ease of being together, and gratitude sometimes begins simply by allowing something good to reach us.

Other moments will remain difficult. Prayer may feel distant, sorrow may resist consolation, and questions may accompany you longer than you expected. Bring them with you, and seek the companionship that helps you carry them. You can speak to God from within uncertainty, offering a few words or a silence that holds more than you know how to express.

You may wish to carry one small practice from this journey: a prayer upon waking, a pause before answering, or a word of thanks as evening comes. Give it a gentle place within your routine, leaving room for it to change as your circumstances change. Let it help you become available to the life unfolding around you and to the love you hope to express within it.

May you discover room for communion in the hours you once hurried through. May familiar tasks become occasions for care, and may beauty interrupt you often enough to awaken gratitude. When you are overwhelmed, may you find the courage to ask for help and the freedom to receive it. When you have caused hurt, may mercy steady you for the work of making amends.

May your prayer grow honest and your attention grow tender. May you become curious about the people you encounter, remembering that each carries a life as deep and unfinished as your own. May you welcome joy without needing to hold it still, and meet sorrow with whatever companionship the day can provide.

And as you return to the life before you, may the remembrance of God arise in small, unforced ways. A breath, a glance, a quiet thank-you, an act of patience: let these become familiar expressions of a relationship still growing. May you find yourself increasingly at home in divine love, ready to receive its kindness and offer it through the ordinary work of your hands.

Go gently into your day. There is someone to love, something to notice, and room to begin again.
