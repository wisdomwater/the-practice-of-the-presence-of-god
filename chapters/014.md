## Day 14 - Freedom from Being Seen

We may discover how much we wanted recognition only when it fails to arrive. A task required patience, someone benefited from our effort, and the day continued without acknowledgment. Alongside our satisfaction in helping, we feel a small ache at having gone unnoticed.

Brother Lawrence described doing ordinary work for the love of God, gradually releasing the desire to please people. His teaching asks us to consider whose approval gives our actions their meaning. When praise becomes necessary to sustain our willingness, even generous work can leave us anxious and resentful.

The longing to be appreciated deserves tenderness. We are relational creatures, and encouragement helps us flourish; we may also need to speak honestly when our contributions are consistently overlooked. Within that human need, we can explore a deeper freedom: the capacity to offer care without requiring each act to confirm our worth.

A kindness can fulfill its purpose quietly in another person's life. Someone is fed, a burden is shared, or a necessary task is completed, whether anyone notices who made it possible. As we offer such actions to God, we can let ourselves belong to the goodness they create, receiving the quiet satisfaction of having participated in love.

**Practice**

Choose one modest act of care that you can offer without drawing attention to it. Make it something freely given and within your limits, rather than another responsibility added to an already crowded day. Before acting, acknowledge any wish to be noticed.

Offer that wish to God as honestly as you offer the action itself. Afterward, attend to the good the act made possible. If disappointment arises because no one thanks you, receive the feeling gently. Let it show you something about your need for appreciation, while allowing this particular kindness to remain complete in the care it provided.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *I am known by You.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Let my care flow freely.*
