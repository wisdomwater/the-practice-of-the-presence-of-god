## Day 5 - Welcomed in Our Imperfection

Many of us carry an image of the person we believe we should become. We imagine someone more capable, organized, patient, and dependable, someone who moves through life without repeatedly stumbling over the same weaknesses. When our actual experience falls short of this imagined ideal, we may become frustrated or ashamed, especially when our mistakes affect the people around us. Over time, we can begin to believe that our worth depends upon how successfully we overcome the parts of ourselves we find disappointing.

Brother Lawrence was remarkably candid about his own limitations. Before entering the monastery, he described himself as an awkward servant who seemed to break almost everything he handled. He expected that monastic life would bring further correction and hardship, perhaps even the punishment he believed his shortcomings deserved. Instead, he discovered a life marked by unexpected satisfaction, finding that his experience of God was more generous than he had anticipated.

There is a meaningful difference between recognizing our imperfections and allowing them to define our identity. Awareness of our limitations can encourage humility, responsibility, and a willingness to grow, but shame often turns that awareness into a judgment against our very existence. When we imagine that God relates to us primarily through disappointment, even our attempts at spiritual growth can become exercises in self-punishment.

Brother Lawrence's experience invites us to reconsider the expectations we bring into our relationship with God. Perhaps we have come expecting condemnation where grace is waiting to meet us, or demanding perfection from ourselves before allowing the possibility of joy. Our shortcomings may still require attention, patience, and change, but they need not prevent us from receiving love along the way.

**Practice**

Notice a moment today when you become frustrated with yourself. Perhaps you make a mistake, forget something important, or respond to another person with less patience than you intended.

Before rushing into self-criticism, pause and acknowledge what happened with kindness and honesty. Consider whether the situation calls for an apology, a practical correction, or simply an acceptance of your human limitations. Then imagine bringing this imperfect moment into God's presence without hiding or defending yourself.

You do not need to excuse your actions or condemn yourself for them. Allow grace to make room for responsibility, learning, and a gentler relationship with yourself.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *I am held in Your love.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I release my self-judgment.*
