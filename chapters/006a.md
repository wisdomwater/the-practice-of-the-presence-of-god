## Second Conversation — Doing All Things for the Love of God

*September 28, 1666*

Brother Lawrence explained that he had long sought to make the love of God the guiding purpose of everything he did. Rather than pursuing spiritual rewards or seeking some benefit for himself, he had resolved to act simply out of love for God, and he found deep satisfaction in this way of living. Even the smallest action could become an expression of devotion. He spoke of taking pleasure in picking up a single straw from the ground for the love of God, desiring nothing in return, not even the gifts or consolations that God might offer.

Yet his spiritual journey had not always been characterized by such freedom and peace. For four years, Brother Lawrence had suffered under the overwhelming conviction that he was condemned and would ultimately be lost. His certainty was so deeply rooted that he believed no one in the world could have persuaded him otherwise. During this period, he endured considerable inward anguish, struggling beneath the weight of what he believed to be his eternal fate.

Eventually, he came to a resolution that transformed his relationship with this fear. He reminded himself that he had entered religious life for the love of God and had sincerely endeavored to direct his actions toward Him. Whether he was ultimately saved or lost, he determined that he would continue loving God for as long as he lived. Even if his eternal destiny remained uncertain, he could at least spend his remaining days offering whatever love he possessed, without allowing the fear of punishment to determine his devotion.

From that time forward, Brother Lawrence experienced a remarkable freedom of spirit and a continuing sense of joy. He remained deeply conscious of his sins and shortcomings, sometimes bringing them before God as evidence of how little he deserved the blessings he received. Yet God, as he understood his experience, continued to pour out grace and kindness in abundance. The contrast between his own unworthiness and the generosity of divine love became a source of wonder rather than despair.

Brother Lawrence believed that this freedom could grow through the simple practice of speaking with God throughout the day. At first, cultivating such awareness required deliberate effort, as the mind was accustomed to wandering among countless concerns. Yet with patient attention, the practice gradually became more natural, until love itself seemed to awaken the desire for communion without requiring the same conscious struggle. What began as an intentional discipline could eventually become a familiar and spontaneous movement of the heart.

Although he had enjoyed many years of spiritual consolation, Brother Lawrence did not assume that his life would remain free from suffering. He expected that difficulties would eventually come, but he was not troubled by anticipating them. He had learned to recognize his inability to sustain himself through his own strength and trusted that God would provide what was needed when the time arrived. Rather than attempting to prepare himself for every possible hardship, he rested in confidence that divine assistance would accompany the circumstances he actually encountered.

This dependence upon God extended even to the ordinary challenges of his daily responsibilities. Whenever an opportunity arose to exercise patience, courage, or some other virtue, he would turn inwardly toward God and acknowledge his need for help. He would admit that he could not accomplish the task unless God enabled him, and he often found himself receiving more strength than he had expected. In this way, his limitations became occasions for prayer, inviting him to participate in what he believed God was accomplishing through him.

He approached his failures with the same simplicity. When he neglected a responsibility or acted contrary to what he believed was right, he acknowledged his fault directly before God without attempting to excuse himself. He confessed that, if left entirely to his own resources, he would likely continue making the same mistakes, and he asked God both to prevent future failures and to correct what had gone wrong. Once he had made this confession, he did not continue troubling himself with anxious regret, but peacefully returned to his ordinary practice of love and adoration.

Brother Lawrence encouraged this same honesty and simplicity in all our dealings with God. He believed we should speak freely and plainly, without needing elaborate words or formal expressions, bringing our needs before God as they arise. Whether facing a difficult task, an unexpected problem, or a moment of uncertainty, we could ask for help as naturally as we might turn toward a trusted companion. He had found through his own experience that such prayers were met with the assistance he needed.

To illustrate this, Brother Lawrence described a recent journey to Burgundy, where he had been sent to purchase wine for the monastery. The assignment was particularly unwelcome because he had little natural ability for business and suffered from a physical disability that made moving about difficult. While aboard the boat, he was unable to walk among the wine casks normally and had to maneuver himself by rolling over them. Despite these obstacles, he refused to become consumed by anxiety about his limitations or the outcome of the purchase.

Instead, he entrusted the responsibility to God, regarding the work as something undertaken in God's service. He carried out his duties as best he could, leaving the results in divine hands, and the business was completed successfully. He recalled a similar journey to Auvergne the previous year, undertaken for the same purpose, which had also turned out well. He could scarcely explain how everything had worked out, but he regarded these experiences as further evidence of God's faithful assistance.

He spoke in much the same way about his years working in the monastery kitchen. By temperament, he had strongly disliked kitchen work, yet he had spent fifteen years performing its demanding and repetitive responsibilities. During that time, he gradually accustomed himself to doing everything for the love of God, asking for grace whenever difficulties arose and seeking to carry out his duties faithfully. Through this practice, work that had once seemed disagreeable became increasingly manageable, and he found that the demands of the kitchen no longer disturbed his inward communion.

Brother Lawrence was satisfied with the responsibilities he now held, but he had no desire to cling to them. He believed he could leave his present position as readily as he had left the kitchen, because his happiness no longer depended upon the particular work assigned to him. Whether engaged in a task he enjoyed or one he naturally disliked, he sought the same opportunity to express his love for God through small acts of faithfulness. In this way, the changing circumstances of his work became less important than the spirit in which he approached them.

He also explained that he experienced little distinction between formal times of prayer and the other hours of his day. He faithfully observed the periods of retirement and prayer required by his religious superior, but he did not feel a particular need to withdraw from his responsibilities in order to experience God's presence. Even his busiest work did not prevent him from maintaining an inward awareness of divine companionship. For him, prayer had become a continuous relationship that accompanied his activities rather than an experience confined to particular times and places.

Because he understood his spiritual responsibility as simply loving God in all things, Brother Lawrence felt little need for a spiritual director to guide his daily practice. He did, however, recognize his need for a confessor who could grant absolution for his sins. He remained keenly aware of his faults, but he did not allow that awareness to lead him into discouragement. Instead, he confessed his wrongdoing openly before God, without defending or excusing himself, and then quietly resumed his familiar practice of love and worship.

Looking back upon his earlier years of spiritual anguish, Brother Lawrence recalled that he had sought no human counsel during that difficult period. He had relied upon the light of faith, trusting that God was present even when his own understanding and emotions offered little reassurance. His response had been to direct his actions toward God, sincerely desiring to please Him regardless of what might eventually become of him. This willingness to continue loving amid uncertainty had become the foundation of his inward freedom.

Brother Lawrence also warned against allowing useless thoughts to occupy the mind. He believed that much spiritual disturbance began when we entertained thoughts unrelated to our present responsibilities or our relationship with God, allowing them to draw us away from inward communion. When such thoughts appeared, he encouraged us to recognize their unhelpful nature and gently turn our attention back toward God. The purpose was to preserve a heart increasingly attentive to divine presence rather than allowing the mind to wander endlessly among distractions.

He acknowledged that this had been particularly difficult during the early years of his practice. At times, almost the entirety of his appointed prayer had been spent struggling with wandering thoughts, repeatedly becoming distracted and attempting to return his attention to God. He had initially practiced forms of meditation, but over time these gradually fell away in a manner he could not fully explain. He found himself unable to organize his devotion according to the established methods that seemed helpful to others, and his own spiritual life developed along a simpler and more direct path.

This experience led Brother Lawrence to reflect upon the purpose of religious disciplines and bodily mortifications. He did not regard these practices as valuable merely because they demanded effort or sacrifice. Their true worth, he believed, depended upon whether they helped the soul grow toward union with God through love. Having considered the matter carefully, he became convinced that the most direct path was to turn continually toward God and perform every action for His sake.

He distinguished between the activity of the understanding and the movement of the will. Intellectual reflection could provide knowledge about God, but Brother Lawrence regarded the loving orientation of the heart as far more important. A person might devote considerable effort to studying spiritual matters or undertaking difficult religious exercises, yet these accomplishments would have little value if they did not lead to love. For him, the essential work of the spiritual life was to love God and find delight in His presence.

He went so far as to say that even the severest forms of self-denial could not remove a single sin if they were separated from the love of God. Forgiveness, he believed, was to be received through the blood of Jesus Christ rather than earned through personal suffering or religious achievement. He encouraged a simple and wholehearted devotion, grounded in confidence that God's mercy was sufficient for human weakness. Indeed, Brother Lawrence observed that some of God's greatest favors seemed to have been given to those who had sinned most deeply, allowing divine mercy to become especially visible through their lives.

Through his years of spiritual practice, Brother Lawrence had come to experience inward joys and sufferings that seemed greater than any pleasure or hardship he had known through outward circumstances. These experiences had so transformed his priorities that he no longer felt consumed by ordinary worries or fears. His deepest desire was simply to remain faithful to God and avoid anything that would offend Him. Everything else had become secondary to the relationship that increasingly shaped his life.

This freedom was particularly evident in the way he responded to his own conduct. When he failed in his duties, he readily acknowledged the failure, recognizing that such weakness was familiar to him and that he depended upon God for the strength to change. When he succeeded, he did not congratulate himself or claim the accomplishment as evidence of his own goodness, but gave thanks to God for the grace that had enabled him. Whether facing success or failure, he returned to the same posture of humility, gratitude, and love, finding his peace in the presence of the One to whom he had entrusted his life.
