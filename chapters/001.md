## Day 1 - The God Who Is Already Here

We often imagine that drawing closer to God requires us to become something more than we presently are. We picture a future version of ourselves who is more patient, more disciplined, less distracted, and better prepared for spiritual intimacy. Perhaps we believe that once we have quieted our minds, overcome our weaknesses, or arranged our lives into something more orderly, we will finally be able to experience God's presence. Until then, we may feel that God remains somewhere beyond our reach, waiting for us to become ready.

Brother Lawrence discovered a different way of approaching the spiritual life. As a seventeenth-century Carmelite lay brother, he spent much of his time attending to ordinary responsibilities, including the humble work of a monastery kitchen. Yet within these seemingly unremarkable activities, he cultivated a growing awareness of God's companionship. He came to understand that communion with God was possible amid the demands and distractions of everyday life, without requiring extraordinary circumstances or uninterrupted solitude.

There is something quietly liberating about this possibility. If God is truly present throughout creation, then we do not need to manufacture that presence through our own spiritual efforts. Our attention may wander, our emotions may fluctuate, and our circumstances may sometimes make prayer feel difficult, but none of these experiences necessarily places us beyond the reach of divine love. Perhaps the beginning of spiritual growth involves learning to recognize what has been patiently available to us all along.

As we begin this journey, we are invited to become curious about the presence of God within the life we already inhabit. We can bring our questions, imperfections, desires, and uncertainties without pretending to be further along than we are. Over time, the ordinary moments of our days may become openings through which we discover a deeper sense of belonging. And perhaps, as our awareness grows, we will begin to recognize that the love we have been searching for has been accompanying us even through the moments when we were unaware of it.

**Practice**

At some point today, pause during an ordinary activity. It might be while preparing a meal, sitting at your desk, walking between rooms, or waiting for something to finish. Without changing what you are doing, gently bring your attention to the possibility that God is present with you in this moment.

Notice your surroundings, the sensations of your body, and the simple rhythm of your breathing. You do not need to create a particular feeling or experience anything extraordinary. Simply allow yourself a moment of openness. If your attention wanders, you can return without judgment.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *You are here with me.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I rest in Your presence.*
