## Day 46 - A Friendship We Can Enter Honestly

When something troubles us, we often know whom we would like to tell. We imagine a familiar voice, someone who will listen patiently and help us carry what feels difficult. Yet we may hesitate before bringing the same concern to God, unsure whether our feelings belong within prayer.

Brother Lawrence calls God a good and faithful Friend, encouraging his correspondent to deepen her confidence in Him. His language gives trust a personal character. We are invited to approach One whose goodness allows us to speak openly about the life we are actually living.

Such friendship can make room for more than composed expressions of faith. We may arrive with disappointment, confusion, affection, or a question that has remained unanswered for years. Learning to pray honestly includes allowing these experiences to be acknowledged without first making them easier to explain.

Our confidence may develop gradually, especially where previous relationships have made trust difficult. We can begin with something small and true, leaving room for silence afterward. Alongside the companionship we receive from other people, prayer can become a place where more of ourselves is welcomed into conversation, and where we learn to receive love with less fear of revealing our need.

**Practice**

Choose one concern you would ordinarily share with a trusted friend. Bring it to God in the same plain language, without turning it into a formal prayer. Describe what happened, how you feel, and what you wish were different.

If you notice yourself editing out anger, uncertainty, or embarrassment, gently acknowledge that impulse. Offer one sentence you might otherwise have withheld. Then remain quiet for a few moments without requiring an answer. Consider whether a conversation with another person would also help. Allow prayer and human companionship to support your willingness to be known and cared for.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Faithful Friend, receive me.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I bring You what is true.*
