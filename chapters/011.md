## Day 11 - When Prayer Feels Dry

We sometimes settle into prayer hoping to recover a familiar feeling of closeness. We become quiet, speak the words that once moved us, and wait for something within us to soften. When little seems to happen, we may wonder whether we have become distracted, lost our way, or somehow disappointed God.

Brother Lawrence acknowledged that he sometimes left his appointed times of private devotion with a dryness of spirit. He could feel more united to God while carrying out ordinary responsibilities than during those quieter hours. Even a life deeply shaped by prayer contained differences he could neither arrange nor entirely explain.

His honesty gives us room to receive our own experience without immediately turning it into a verdict. Affection between people also moves through changing moods, seasons, and degrees of felt closeness. We continue listening, caring, and making ourselves available because the relationship matters, even when its warmth is difficult to feel.

We can bring that same patient sincerity into prayer. A quiet moment may hold only a few honest words, followed by the ordinary work of the day. God may meet us through another person's need, a familiar responsibility, or a simple willingness to remain open while our feelings offer little reassurance.

**Practice**

Set aside a few quiet minutes without choosing a feeling you hope to produce. Notice what is present: warmth, restlessness, weariness, peace, or perhaps very little you can name. Speak honestly to God about what you find, allowing your prayer to be as simple as, “This is how I am today.”

When the time ends, move gently into an ordinary activity. Attend to the person or task before you without searching for evidence that the prayer worked. If you notice kindness, beauty, or a small opportunity to care, receive it. Let your relationship with God accompany the day as it unfolds.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Here I am, God.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I remain open to You.*
