## Day 21 - Receiving the Time Before Us

We often leave meaningful things for a day when we expect to have more room. We will make the call, offer the apology, or become more attentive once the current demands have eased. Meanwhile, the ordinary days continue passing, carrying opportunities we may not recognize until afterward.

Brother Lawrence's second letter speaks urgently about the brevity of life. He urges his reader to receive the present time of grace and remember that death will come. His warning carries the severity of his convictions, asking us to consider how much we postpone while assuming that time will remain available.

Such reflection can bring both sorrow and clarity. We may grieve what we have neglected, while also recognizing someone we can still love or a responsibility we can still meet. The life before us today contains possibilities that deserve our care, however limited our energy or imperfect our circumstances.

We can respond through one sincere action within reach. A conversation may begin, a kindness may be offered, or a few quiet minutes may finally receive our attention. Entrusting what has passed to God's mercy, we can ask for help in receiving this day and participating in the good it makes possible.

**Practice**

Consider one meaningful thing you have been postponing because you assume there will be a better time. Choose something modest enough to approach today: contacting a friend, acknowledging a hurt, expressing affection, or making space for prayer.

Notice what has made you hesitate, and respond with patience toward your actual limitations. Then take one available step, even if the whole matter cannot be resolved. Afterward, offer any regret about lost time to God. Let the action belong to this day without requiring it to compensate for everything that came before. Allow yourself to receive the time you have been given.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Thank You for this day.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Help me receive it fully.*
