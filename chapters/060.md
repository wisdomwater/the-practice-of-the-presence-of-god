## Day 60 - A Life Open to God

As a journey draws to a close, we often look for evidence that it has changed us. We remember what moved us and notice how much remains difficult. There may be gratitude alongside the familiar wish that we had become more attentive, more peaceful, or more consistent by now.

In his final letter, Brother Lawrence invites his reader to make the heart a spiritual temple. Written as he anticipates death, his counsel gathers the ordinary practice that has shaped his life: humble conversation, loving attention, and dependence upon grace. The relationship he cherishes remains his invitation to another person.

We can carry that invitation beyond these readings without needing to settle an account of our progress. Our hearts will still hold distraction, sorrow, affection, and unfinished hopes. All of this can enter the conversation as we learn to make room for God's presence within the lives we already inhabit.

Tomorrow will bring its own work, encounters, and opportunities to care. We can begin there, offering what is ours to do and receiving help where we need it. The words of this book may fall quiet, while the relationship they have encouraged continues through meals, conversations, moments of rest, and the small, sincere acts through which we answer love.

**Practice**

Look back over these sixty days and choose one invitation you would like to carry forward. It may be a brief prayer, a gentle return after distraction, an ordinary task offered with love, or a willingness to receive care.

Connect it with a familiar part of your day so that it has somewhere to take root. Keep the intention modest enough to welcome again after you forget. Then offer a prayer of gratitude for whatever you have received, including anything still unfolding beyond your understanding. Close the reading and turn toward the life before you. Let one ordinary act become your next response.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Make my heart Your dwelling.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Let my life answer in love.*
