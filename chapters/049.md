## Day 49 - Strength for What Remains

When suffering continues, our prayers often return to the same request: please let this end. We long for relief, for an explanation, or simply for a morning when the burden feels lighter. As the days accumulate, we may struggle to imagine what prayer can offer while the answer we desire remains beyond reach.

Brother Lawrence tells his correspondent that he is praying for strength and patience rather than deliverance. His counsel arises from a demanding understanding of surrender, in which suffering belongs within God's purposes. We may find that understanding difficult while still receiving his concern for the strength she needs today.

Our own prayer can hold both the desire for relief and the need for endurance. We can ask for healing, seek help, and speak honestly about exhaustion. Alongside those requests, we can ask for whatever will sustain the next hour: enough patience to receive care, enough courage to name a need, or enough rest to continue.

We do not have to imagine enduring every possible tomorrow. The present moment offers a smaller place in which to ask for help. Within it, prayer may become the simple willingness to be accompanied while something painful remains unresolved and our own resources feel uncertain.

**Practice**

Name one difficulty for which you desire relief. Tell God plainly what you wish would change, without softening the request or explaining why you should be more accepting.

Then consider what would help you bear the next part of the day. Perhaps you need rest, assistance with a task, a conversation, or permission to leave something unfinished. Ask God for that support, and take one available step toward receiving it. If you have no clear words, pray, “Help me with this hour.” Let your prayer remain close to your present need, without requiring you to promise strength for the days ahead.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Meet me in this hour.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Give me strength to receive help.*
