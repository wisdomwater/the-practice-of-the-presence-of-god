## Thirteenth Letter — Faith When God Seems Hidden

*To the Reverend Mother*  
*November 28, 1690*

My Good Mother,

If we were thoroughly accustomed to practicing God's presence, I believe our bodily afflictions would be greatly eased. God often permits us to suffer for a time to purify our souls and draw us into a more continual companionship with Him. I find it difficult to understand how a soul that remains with God and desires Him alone can still feel pain. Yet my own experience leaves me in no doubt that it can.

Take courage. Continue offering Him your sorrows and asking for the strength to endure them. Above all, cultivate the habit of speaking with God continually, remembering Him as often as you can.

Worship Him amid your infirmities, and from time to time offer yourself to Him anew. At the height of your suffering, ask humbly and lovingly for His help, as a child turns to a good father. Ask Him to grant you the assistance of His grace and to bring your will into harmony with His own. I will support you with my poor, faltering prayers.

God has many ways of drawing us toward Himself. Sometimes He conceals His presence from us. At such times, faith alone must sustain us and provide the foundation of our confidence. This faith will not fail us in our need, and all our trust must rest in God.

I do not know what God intends for me, but I find myself increasingly happy. The whole world suffers, while I, who deserve the severest correction, experience joys so constant and so great that I can scarcely contain them. I would gladly ask God to let me share some of your suffering, were I not so conscious of my weakness. If He left me to myself for even a moment, I would become the most miserable person alive.

And yet I can scarcely imagine Him leaving me alone. Faith gives me a conviction of His presence as strong as anything the senses could provide. I am certain that He never forsakes us unless we have first forsaken Him.

Let us therefore be watchful that we do not turn away. Let us remain with Him always, living and dying in His company. Ask this grace for me, as I will ask it for you.

Yours in our Lord.
