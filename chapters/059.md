## Day 59 - Continuing to Knock

A prayer offered for many years can become difficult to speak again. We know the words, remember the hope with which we first said them, and feel the weariness that has gathered around waiting. Sometimes we hesitate because another asking seems likely to awaken another disappointment.

Brother Lawrence closes his final letter with an invitation to keep knocking. He trusts that God can open in a moment what has remained closed for years. His assurance expresses the confidence of his own faith, offered to someone whose long suffering he has followed with concern.

We may receive this encouragement while remaining honest about what we cannot predict. Persistence does not give us control over the timing or form of an answer. We can continue asking for what matters, alongside seeking help, reconsidering our understanding, and allowing the prayer itself to change as we change.

There may be days when knocking consists of a familiar request and days when it becomes a question or a lament. The conversation can hold all these movements. We bring our desire before God again because it remains part of our life, trusting that even a weary prayer deserves to be heard and that our uncertainty need not bring the relationship to an end.

**Practice**

Recall one prayer you have carried for a long time. Notice whether its words still express what you need or whether your circumstances have changed. Speak it again in the language that feels most truthful today.

Include whatever waiting has brought with it: hope, disappointment, anger, gratitude, or fatigue. Then sit quietly for a few moments without searching for a sign. Consider whether there is a practical step or a conversation that could accompany your asking. If you have no strength to continue today, let someone else's care support you. Allow persistence to include both renewed prayer and needed rest.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Hear the longing I carry.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Keep me open to You.*
