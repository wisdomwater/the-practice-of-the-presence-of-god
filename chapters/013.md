## Day 13 - A Conversation Without Ceremony

Sometimes we hesitate to pray because we cannot find the right words. Our concerns seem too ordinary, our thoughts too scattered, or our feelings too mixed to bring before God. We postpone the conversation until we can become quiet and composed, while the actual circumstances of our lives continue asking for attention.

Brother Lawrence encouraged a familiarity with God that could accompany whatever was happening. Before beginning his work, he asked for assistance and offered what he was about to do. As the work unfolded, he continued speaking inwardly with God, bringing the relationship into the changing needs of each moment.

There is room within this conversation for unfinished thoughts and very simple requests. We can ask for patience before answering a difficult message, admit our reluctance as we begin a responsibility, or give thanks when someone offers kindness. Our ordinary experience gives prayer something honest and particular to hold.

Over time, this way of praying may help us recognize how much of ourselves we have kept outside the conversation. Irritation, uncertainty, affection, and fatigue can all be acknowledged in God's presence. We begin to share the day more freely, trusting that the One who receives us already knows the life we are trying to express.

**Practice**

Choose three ordinary transitions today, such as beginning work, preparing a meal, or returning home. At each transition, pause briefly and tell God something true about the moment. You might say, “I am tired and need patience,” or, “Thank You for bringing me here.”

Let the words arise naturally rather than searching for a particular formula. If you remember God again during the activity, continue the conversation with whatever is present. When the task ends, offer a simple word of thanks. Allow these brief exchanges to become openings through which more of your actual life can enter prayer.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *You are here with me.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I share this day with You.*
