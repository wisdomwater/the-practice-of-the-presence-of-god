## Day 56 - Love Within the Pain

Pain can narrow our world until little seems to exist beyond what hurts. A difficult hour stretches, familiar comforts lose their warmth, and other people's ordinary lives appear strangely distant. We may wonder how love can reach us when suffering occupies so much of our attention.

Brother Lawrence writes that love sweetens pain. He understands affliction as something that can be endured for God's sake, with courage arising through companionship. His language describes a possibility he cherishes, though it may feel far from the experience of someone who is exhausted or afraid.

We can approach his words without requiring suffering to become pleasant. Love may reach us through a hand held quietly, someone who takes our distress seriously, or the freedom to speak without protecting another person from our sorrow. The pain remains, while its place within our experience becomes less solitary.

Our own love may also continue in modest forms. We might receive care gratefully, express affection, or allow someone to know what we need. There can be tenderness within a day we would never have chosen. We can welcome that tenderness as it comes, without judging ourselves when comfort is difficult to feel or when courage consists simply in letting another person stay.

**Practice**

Recall one expression of love that has reached you during a difficult time. Notice its particular shape: a message, a shared meal, practical assistance, or someone's quiet presence. Give thanks for the person and the care they offered.

Then consider whether a similar kindness is available to receive or offer today. Keep your response close to your actual capacity. You may need to ask someone to sit with you, or you may be able to accompany another person briefly. Let the care have value without expecting it to remove the pain. Notice whatever room it creates for tenderness.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Let love reach me here.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Help me receive its tenderness.*
