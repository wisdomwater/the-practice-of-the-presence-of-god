## Eleventh Letter — The Faithful Friend

*To Madame ——*  
*October 29, 1689*

Madame,

I have found it quite difficult to bring myself to write to Monsieur de ——. I am doing so now only because you and Madame de —— have asked me. Please address the letter and send it to him.

I am very glad of the trust you place in God, and I pray that He will deepen it within you more and more. We cannot have too much confidence in so good and faithful a Friend. He will never fail us, either in this life or in the life to come.

If Monsieur de —— learns to benefit spiritually from the loss he has suffered and places all his confidence in God, God will soon provide him with another friend, more powerful and more willing to help him. God directs human hearts as He pleases. Perhaps Monsieur de —— had become too attached to the friend he has lost. We should love our friends, while ensuring that our affection for them does not displace the love of God, which must hold first place.

Please remember the counsel I have so often given you: think of God frequently, by day and by night, during your work and even in your recreation. He is always near you and accompanies you everywhere. Do not leave Him alone.

You would consider it discourteous to neglect a friend who had come to visit you. Why, then, should God remain without your attention? Remember Him often, offer Him your worship continually, and live and die in His company.

This is the glorious calling of a Christian. It is the very life we have undertaken, and if we do not yet know how to live it, we must learn.

I will do my best to support you with my prayers.

Yours in our Lord.
