## Day 17 - Learning to Approach

The way we imagine someone receiving us shapes what we are willing to reveal. If we expect irritation, we may rehearse our explanation before speaking. If we anticipate kindness, we can more readily admit confusion, ask for help, and bring forward the parts of ourselves we would otherwise conceal.

In his first letter, Brother Lawrence described approaching God through more than one image. Sometimes he saw himself as a guilty person before a judge; at other times, he encountered God within his heart as his Father. His prayer held both an awareness of wrongdoing and the possibility of intimate belonging.

We may recognize a similar range within ourselves. Some days we approach readily; on others, even beginning to pray awakens the fear of being unwelcome. We can bring that fear into the conversation, allowing God to receive our uncertainty about the very relationship we are seeking.

Brother Lawrence found that repeated attention gradually nurtured a holy freedom and familiarity. We might begin to cultivate that familiarity by speaking one unguarded sentence at a time. As we bring our actual lives before God, we make room to discover how honesty, responsibility, and the experience of being loved can grow together.

**Practice**

Before praying today, pause and notice how you expect God to receive you. You might imagine welcome, disappointment, distance, or something difficult to name. Acknowledge whatever arises without trying to replace it with a more acceptable feeling.

Then speak one honest sentence about your present life. You could name something you regret, something you need, or something for which you feel grateful. If the word “Father” helps you approach, use it gently; otherwise, address God in language that allows sincerity. Remain for a few moments without composing an explanation. Let your honest presence be enough to begin the conversation.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Receive me as I am.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Teach my heart to trust.*
