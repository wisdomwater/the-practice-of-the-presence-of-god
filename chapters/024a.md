## Fourth Letter — Persevering in Holy Freedom

*To the Reverend Mother*  
*November 3, 1685*

My Reverend and Greatly Honored Mother,

I have received the prayer beads you entrusted to the young lady who brought them to me. I am surprised that you have not shared your thoughts about the little book I sent, which surely must have reached you. Please give yourself wholeheartedly to the practice it describes, even in your old age. It is better to begin late than never to begin at all.

I find it difficult to understand how people devoted to religious life can remain satisfied without practicing the presence of God. For my part, as far as I am able, I withdraw inwardly to be with Him in the very center of my soul. While I remain there with Him, I fear no evil. Even the slightest turning away from Him becomes unbearable to me.

This practice places little strain upon the body. Nevertheless, I believe it is fitting to deny ourselves certain small pleasures from time to time, and even frequently, although those pleasures may be innocent and entirely permissible. God, as I understand it, will not allow a soul that desires to belong wholly to Him to find its pleasures apart from Him. It seems only reasonable that such a soul should seek its enjoyment in His company.

Yet I do not mean that we should live under harsh constraint. We must serve God with a holy freedom, carrying out our responsibilities faithfully and without anxiety or agitation. Whenever we notice that our attention has wandered, we should bring it back to Him gently and peacefully. Our work and our inward communion can continue together in this spirit.

We must place our whole trust in God, setting aside other anxieties and even certain particular forms of devotion. These practices may be very good in themselves, yet we sometimes undertake them without considering whether they serve their purpose. They are means of drawing near to God. When the practice of His presence has brought us into His company, we need not leave that communion merely to return to the means by which we sought it.

Remaining in His holy presence, we can continue our loving exchange in many ways. At one moment, we may offer adoration or praise; at another, express our longing for Him. We may make an offering, give thanks, or turn toward Him in whatever sincere movement of love arises within us. There are many ways for the heart to remain engaged with God.

Do not become discouraged by the resistance you may feel within yourself. You will need to make a determined effort against that reluctance, even when doing so feels forceful. At the beginning, it can seem as though the time spent in this practice has been wasted. Continue nevertheless, resolving to persevere for the rest of your life despite the difficulties you encounter.

I entrust myself to the prayers of your holy community, and especially to yours.

Yours in our Lord.
