## Twelfth Letter — Companionship in Affliction

*To the Reverend Mother*  
*November 17, 1690*

My Reverend and Greatly Honored Mother,

I am not praying that your troubles will be taken away. Rather, I earnestly ask God to give you strength and patience to bear them for as long as He permits. Seek your consolation in the One who, as I understand it, holds you upon the cross and will release you when He judges the time right. Blessed are those who suffer in His company.

Accustom yourself to remaining with Him in your suffering, asking Him for the strength to endure as much and as long as He considers necessary. Those whose love is fixed upon the world do not understand this, which does not surprise me. They experience suffering as people attached to the world rather than as those who love Christ. They regard illness only as a bodily affliction, without recognizing in it a gift from God, and therefore find only sorrow and distress.

Those who receive illness as coming from God's hand, however, understanding it as an expression of His mercy and a means through which He works for their salvation, often discover great sweetness and felt consolation within it. I wish you could become convinced that God is often nearer to us, and His presence more powerfully at work within us, during illness than during health.

I believe that He has reserved your healing to Himself, and for that reason I urge you to rely upon Him as your physician above all others. Place your whole confidence in Him. I believe you will soon experience the effects of that trust, effects we often delay by placing greater confidence in medicine than in God.

Whatever remedies you use, they can benefit you only insofar as He allows. When suffering comes from God, He alone can bring it to an end. I believe He often sends illnesses of the body to heal the illnesses of the soul. Take comfort, then, in the sovereign Physician of both body and soul.

I imagine you may reply that it is easy for me to speak this way, since I eat and drink at the Lord's table. You are right about the blessings I receive. Yet consider what it would mean for a person guilty of the gravest crimes to sit at his king's table and be served by the king's own hands while remaining uncertain whether he had been pardoned. Would that person feel no anguish?

I believe his distress would be profound, and that only confidence in the goodness of his sovereign could quiet it. In the same way, whatever delights I experience at my King's table, my sins remain before my eyes. The uncertainty of my pardon troubles me, although, strange as it may sound, there is something pleasing even in that anguish.

Be content with the condition in which God has placed you. However happy you may imagine me to be, I envy you. Pain and suffering would be paradise to me if I endured them with my God, while the greatest pleasures would become hell if I could enjoy them apart from Him. My joy would be to have something to suffer for His sake.

Before long, I shall go to God—to render an account of my life before Him. What comforts me now is that I behold Him through faith. This awareness is sometimes so vivid that I could almost say, “I no longer merely believe; I see.” I experience what faith teaches, and in that assurance, continuing to practice faith, I intend to live and die in His company.

Remain always with God. His companionship is your sure support and consolation in affliction. I will ask Him to remain with you.

Please convey my respects to the Reverend Mother Superior, and remember me in your prayers.

Yours in our Lord.
