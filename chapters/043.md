## Day 43 - The Pace of Grace

Once we recognize something that needs to change, we often want the change to happen quickly. We imagine how much easier life would become if we could finally respond with patience, release resentment, or remain faithful to what matters. Our longing for growth can gradually become impatience with the person who still needs to grow.

Brother Lawrence describes a sincere sister who wants to move faster than grace. He recognizes her goodwill while reminding his correspondent that holiness does not arrive all at once. She needs encouragement, wise counsel, and the steady example of people learning to love alongside her.

His observation invites us to examine the demands we place upon our own development. We may be asking one decision to undo habits formed over many years. When familiar difficulties return, we can seek the help and understanding that make a different response more possible.

There is meaningful work available at the pace we can actually sustain. We can acknowledge harm, practice a small kindness, and remain willing to learn from someone else's wisdom. As we entrust the larger process to God, patience becomes part of our growth, giving our desire for change enough room to become a faithful way of living.

**Practice**

Choose one area in which you feel impatient with your growth. Describe the change you hope for, then notice what you expect yourself to accomplish immediately. Consider whether that expectation allows for learning, support, and the persistence of established habits.

Ask God to help you recognize one realistic step for today. You might prepare for a difficult situation, seek advice, or practice a response you want to become more familiar. Take that step without requiring it to settle the whole matter. If possible, remember someone whose patient example encourages you. Let their life remind you that growth has a history.
