## Day 25 - Beginning in the Life We Have

Certain desires become painful because we have carried them for so long without acting upon them. We wanted to become more prayerful, more attentive, or more available to the people we love. As the years pass, the thought of beginning can awaken regret about the person we might already have become.

Brother Lawrence writes to the Reverend Mother with the plain encouragement that it is better to begin late than never. He urges her to undertake the practice of God's presence in her old age. His invitation gives her remaining life somewhere to turn, even though the years behind her cannot be recovered.

We may be tempted to spend our energy imagining how differently things could have unfolded. Yet the person who can respond to God is the person we are today, shaped by all that has happened. Our experience, weariness, hope, and disappointment can enter the relationship together.

Beginning now may be quieter than the fresh start we once imagined. We might offer a few minutes of attention or bring an overlooked part of our day into prayer. Such a beginning deserves room to grow within our present circumstances, receiving the time before us with enough tenderness to make use of it.

**Practice**

Name one desire for your spiritual life that you have postponed. Notice whether thinking about it brings regret, embarrassment, or the feeling that you should be further along. Bring that response honestly before God.

Then choose a beginning small enough to make today. You might sit quietly for two minutes, pray before starting a familiar task, or take a short walk with an intention to notice God's presence. When you finish, resist expanding the moment into a demanding plan. Let this first response be complete. Tomorrow can receive another small beginning when it comes.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Meet me as I am.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I begin with You today.*
