## Day 26 - A Little More Freedom

We often reach for small pleasures almost before noticing the movement. A quiet interval fills with scrolling, a difficult task sends us searching for distraction, or an uncomfortable feeling prompts another familiar escape. These comforts may be harmless in themselves, yet their familiarity can make it difficult to choose anything else.

Brother Lawrence recommends occasionally relinquishing even innocent pleasures as part of giving ourselves to God. Alongside this demanding counsel, he insists that we serve God in holy freedom. His letter holds together a willingness to surrender and a gentleness that allows us to carry out our responsibilities without anxious strain.

We can explore that tension by noticing how freely we receive and release what we enjoy. A pleasure may refresh us and help us return to life with gratitude. At other times, we cling to it so closely that we become less available to prayer, another person, or a responsibility needing care.

A small, voluntary pause can reveal something about that attachment. We may encounter restlessness, disappointment, or an unexpected sense of room. Bringing those responses to God allows relinquishment to become part of an honest relationship, through which we gradually learn to enjoy good things with greater freedom.

**Practice**

Choose one minor, optional pleasure you tend to reach for automatically, such as checking your phone during a quiet moment. Once today, gently delay it for a few minutes. Keep the experiment modest and freely chosen.

Notice what arises during the pause. You might feel bored, restless, relieved, or eager to return to the familiar activity. Tell God what you notice, then attend to something nearby: your surroundings, the person with you, or a task awaiting care. Afterward, decide whether to resume the pleasure. Let the experience help you understand your attachment without turning it into a verdict about yourself.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *You know what I seek.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Teach me to choose freely.*
