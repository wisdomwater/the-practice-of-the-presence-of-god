## Day 9 - Returning After Failure

After we have acted against our own convictions, it can be difficult to know when remorse has done its work. We replay the moment, imagine how we should have behaved, and question whether our regret is deep enough. Returning to ordinary life can feel almost irresponsible, as though continued distress might somehow prove our sincerity.

Brother Lawrence approached his failures with plain acknowledgment. He confessed what he had done, admitted his need for help, and asked God to correct what had gone wrong. Then he resumed his familiar practice of love, trusting that he could continue in relationship while still needing to change.

His simplicity invites us to examine what happens after our own confession. Where someone has been hurt, love calls us toward apology and repair. Where a pattern keeps recurring, we may need support or a practical change that helps us respond differently next time.

Having taken the next available step, we can allow ourselves to receive mercy. Brother Lawrence understood forgiveness as a gift received through Christ, beyond anything personal suffering could earn. We can trust that gift enough to become available again: to listen, to care, and to offer the next moment with greater honesty about both our weakness and our hope.

**Practice**

Bring to mind a recent failure that you can consider without becoming overwhelmed. Describe what happened plainly, keeping your attention on the action and its consequences. Speak to God about what you regret and where you need help.

Consider whether something remains to be done. An apology, a repaired commitment, or a change in your surroundings may give your desire to grow a concrete expression. Take that step, or make a realistic plan for it. Then let yourself return to the day. If self-accusation resumes, gently remember that you have acknowledged the failure and begun responding to it.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *I receive Your mercy.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Help me begin again.*
