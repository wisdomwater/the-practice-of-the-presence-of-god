## Day 33 - In the Sculptor’s Hands

Our efforts to change can become exhausting when we assume that every part of the work depends upon us. We observe our reactions, identify our shortcomings, and try to manage ourselves into greater patience or generosity. Even prayer can become another occasion for measuring the distance between who we are and who we hope to become.

Brother Lawrence sometimes imagined himself as a stone in the hands of a sculptor. He presented himself to God and asked that the divine image be formed within his soul. This prayer gave expression to a deep willingness to be changed by a wisdom beyond his own.

There is vulnerability in offering ourselves this way. We may discover that growth requires an apology we have avoided, help we have resisted, or freedom from an ambition we have mistaken for faithfulness. We remain participants in the process, responding through the choices that become available to us.

The sculptor's image also leaves room for patience. We rarely understand the whole shape of a life while we are living it, and we cannot force every change to arrive at once. We can offer ourselves again today, asking God to shape our capacity for love through the ordinary opportunities and honest responses within reach.

**Practice**

Sit quietly with your hands open, if that posture is comfortable. Imagine placing your unfinished life before God, including both what you appreciate and what you wish were different. Pray, “Form Your image within me.”

Choose one quality you hope will grow, such as patience, courage, or compassion. Consider where today's circumstances might invite a small response that expresses it. You might listen before defending yourself, ask for needed help, or speak kindly during frustration. Make that response your offering. Leave the larger work unfinished, trusting that you can return tomorrow with the same willingness to be shaped.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Hold me in Your wisdom.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Shape my heart in love.*
