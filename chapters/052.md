## Day 52 - When Presence Is Hard to Feel

During a difficult season, we may reach for the comfort that once came readily and find ourselves unable to feel it. Familiar prayers seem quiet, and words about God's nearness meet an inward emptiness. Alongside our original sorrow, we begin to wonder what this absence means about our relationship with Him.

Brother Lawrence acknowledges that God sometimes seems hidden. Although his letter describes extraordinary joy and confidence, he makes room for faith that must continue without the same felt reassurance. His counsel invites us to consider what might sustain relationship when our experience offers little encouragement.

We may struggle with his understanding that God deliberately conceals Himself. We do not have to settle that question before speaking honestly about the distance we feel. A prayer such as “I cannot sense You here” can express our longing without pretending that the longing has already been answered.

Trust may take a modest form during such times. We can receive the care of another person, remain open to a familiar prayer, or simply allow our questions to be present before God. The silence may continue, yet we can give ourselves permission to seek companionship within it without treating our inability to feel comfort as a failure.

**Practice**

Set aside a brief moment to acknowledge how God's presence feels to you today. Use an honest word or phrase: close, uncertain, distant, comforting, or difficult to imagine. Let that description become the beginning of your prayer.

If you feel distance, speak about it without trying to correct the feeling. You might pray, “I miss the comfort I once knew,” or, “Help me remain open.” Then notice one available form of companionship, whether a trusted person, a familiar passage, or a quiet place. Receive what it offers without requiring it to resolve the silence or prove that your faith is strong.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Receive me in this silence.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Help me remain open.*
