## Day 48 - Welcoming God into Our Enjoyment

It can be easier to remember God when we are worried than when we are enjoying ourselves. Need gives us something immediate to say, while an afternoon with friends or a favorite activity may seem complete without prayer. We quietly reserve spiritual attention for moments of difficulty, duty, or deliberate devotion.

Brother Lawrence encourages remembrance during recreation as well as work. He compares God's presence to that of a visiting friend, asking why we would overlook such company. His image invites us to consider how enjoyment itself might become a setting for relationship.

We can receive the invitation without making ourselves anxious about every moment of forgetfulness. A friend welcomed into our lives shares more than our troubles; there is room for laughter, pleasure, and unhurried companionship. We can turn toward God within these experiences with the same naturalness we are learning to bring into our responsibilities.

Perhaps we pause over the goodness of a meal, notice our gratitude during a game, or receive a beautiful place with wonder. Such remembrance allows pleasure to open outward into appreciation. We begin to share the parts of life we love, discovering that recreation can nourish our communion as we become more attentive to the gifts and people before us.

**Practice**

Choose something enjoyable you expect to do today, such as listening to music, sharing a meal, taking a walk, or spending time with someone. As you begin, briefly acknowledge God's company: “Thank You for this. Let me receive it well.”

Then give yourself to the experience without monitoring your attention. If gratitude arises naturally, let it become a quiet prayer. Notice the people, beauty, or care that make the enjoyment possible. When the activity ends, offer one simple expression of thanks. Allow the practice to deepen your pleasure in what is good and your affection for those who share it.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *You share this joy with me.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I receive it with thanks.*
