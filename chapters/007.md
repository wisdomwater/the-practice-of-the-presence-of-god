## Day 7 - The Smallest Offering

Much of our life is spent doing things that will need to be done again tomorrow. We wash dishes, answer messages, prepare meals, and clear spaces that soon become cluttered. Because these tasks rarely produce anything lasting, we can begin to experience them as interruptions to a more meaningful life we hope eventually to reach.

Brother Lawrence spoke of picking up a single straw from the ground for the love of God. The action was almost weightless, easily overlooked and quickly forgotten. Yet he found within it an opportunity to offer affection, allowing something small to become part of a living relationship with God.

This understanding developed through years of work he had initially disliked. During fifteen years in the monastery kitchen, he learned to bring his reluctance, fatigue, and need for help into his communion with God. The work remained demanding, while his way of inhabiting it gradually changed.

We might begin by considering who receives care through the ordinary things we do. A meal sustains someone; a cleared table offers room to gather; a patient reply makes another person's burden easier to carry. As we attend to these small possibilities, love can give familiar work a depth we had previously missed.

**Practice**

Choose one ordinary task you expect to do today. Before beginning, pause long enough to notice your feelings about it, including any boredom or reluctance. Bring those feelings honestly before God, then offer a simple intention: “May someone receive care through what I am doing.”

Carry out the task at a comfortable pace, paying attention to the people or purposes it serves. If your attention wanders, return when you notice. When you finish, allow the offering to be complete, without examining whether you felt sufficiently prayerful. Let this small act belong to your relationship with God.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Receive this small offering.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *May it carry Your love.*
