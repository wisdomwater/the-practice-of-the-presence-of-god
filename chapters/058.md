## Day 58 - Courage, Gentleness, and Love

When life becomes painful, we may imagine that strength requires us to become harder. We brace ourselves, conceal our distress, and try to prevent anything from reaching the places that already hurt. This can help us get through a moment, yet over time we may miss the tenderness that once made us feel connected to others.

Near the end of his life, Brother Lawrence describes asking for the strength to suffer courageously, gently, and lovingly. His prayer holds together qualities we sometimes separate. Courage gives him a way to endure, while gentleness and love shape how he remains present within the experience.

We may not share his delight in suffering, and we need not imitate feelings that are not our own. We can still receive the intention within his prayer. Even while seeking relief, we can ask for help in meeting pain without allowing it to determine every response we make.

Some days, courage will mean admitting that we are struggling. Gentleness may appear in the way we speak to someone helping us, while love may allow us to receive care without embarrassment. These small responses give suffering a place within relationship, where our vulnerability can be known and our strength can include the willingness to remain tender.

**Practice**

Bring one present difficulty into prayer and consider the three qualities Brother Lawrence names. Ask what courage, gentleness, or love might look like within your actual circumstances.

Choose just one response to practice today. You might speak honestly about your limits, soften an impatient reply, or accept help with something you cannot manage alone. Keep the action small enough to be sincere. If distress overwhelms your intention, pause and receive whatever support is available. Later, acknowledge any moment in which tenderness remained possible. Let it encourage you without requiring yourself to meet every difficult moment in the same way.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Give me courage for today.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Keep my heart tender.*
