## Third Conversation — A Heart Established in Trust

*November 22, 1666*

Brother Lawrence explained that the foundation of his spiritual life had been a deep reverence for God, grounded in faith. Once this understanding had taken root within him, his chief concern was to turn away promptly from thoughts that drew him elsewhere, so that everything he did might be offered for the love of God. When he realized that he had gone a considerable time without remembering God, he did not become distressed. He acknowledged his weakness before Him and returned with renewed trust, allowing the discovery of his forgetfulness to deepen his dependence upon divine goodness.

He believed that placing our trust in God greatly honored Him and opened us to abundant grace. God, he insisted, could never deceive those who entrusted themselves to Him. He was also convinced that God would not allow a soul to remain long in suffering when it had surrendered itself completely to Him and resolved to endure everything for His sake.

Brother Lawrence had experienced the timely assistance of divine grace so often that he no longer approached his responsibilities with the anxious anticipation that had once troubled him. When the time came to act, he found in God, as though looking into a clear mirror, an understanding of what the situation required. He described this as a relatively recent freedom, contrasting it with earlier years when his affairs had occupied him with considerable worry. Experience had gradually taught him to trust that the help he needed would be available when his duties actually arose.

His attention had become so steadily directed toward God that he gave little thought to his actions once they were completed, and sometimes scarcely reflected upon them while performing them. He remarked that, on leaving the table, he might not remember what he had eaten. Through all these ordinary activities, he held to one intention: to do everything for the love of God. He thanked God for guiding these actions and the countless others that had filled his life, carrying out his responsibilities with a simplicity that kept him established in an awareness of divine companionship.

When outward business briefly drew his attention away, he sometimes experienced a renewed remembrance of God that seemed to come as a gift. This awareness would fill him with such ardent love and joy that he found it difficult to contain his response. Indeed, he often felt more closely united to God during his ordinary occupations than when he withdrew from them for private devotion. He acknowledged that he sometimes emerged from those periods of retirement with a considerable dryness of spirit.

Although he had enjoyed this awareness of God for many years, Brother Lawrence expected that some great suffering of body or mind might still await him. The hardest possibility he could imagine was losing the felt sense of God's presence that had become so familiar. Nevertheless, he trusted God's goodness enough to believe that he would never be wholly abandoned and would receive the strength to bear whatever suffering God permitted. This confidence left him without fear of what might come.

He also felt little need to seek advice about the condition of his soul. When he had attempted to do so, he said, he had often come away more confused than before. Conscious of his willingness to lay down his life for the love of God, he felt no apprehension about the path he was following. His assurance rested in the sincerity of that love and in the goodness of the One to whom he had entrusted himself.

Brother Lawrence regarded complete surrender to God as a sure way to heaven, a path upon which enough light would always be given to guide one's conduct. At the beginning of the spiritual life, he believed, a person needed to persevere faithfully in duty and self-denial. In time, he said, these efforts were followed by joys beyond expression. Whenever difficulties arose, he encouraged turning to Jesus Christ and asking for His grace, through which even what seemed burdensome could become easy.

He believed that many Christians made little progress because they remained absorbed in penances and particular religious exercises while neglecting the love of God toward which those practices were meant to lead. He thought the consequences could be seen in their conduct, where outward devotion too often failed to produce substantial virtue. Spiritual disciplines had their purpose, but they could not fulfill it when love itself was overlooked.

For Brother Lawrence, approaching God required neither specialized knowledge nor mastery of an elaborate method. What mattered was a heart firmly resolved to turn toward Him, undertaking everything either in direct attention to God or for His sake. The whole movement of his spiritual life rested in this wholehearted desire to love God above all things.
