## Day 28 - The Smallest Remembrance

When life becomes difficult, even the things that usually sustain us can begin to feel demanding. We may want to pray yet struggle to gather our thoughts. The prospect of finding words, setting aside time, or becoming inwardly composed can seem beyond the strength available to us.

Writing to a woman burdened by troubles, Brother Lawrence offers a remarkably modest invitation. She can remember God briefly, ask for help, offer her sorrow, or give thanks for a kindness received. Even while eating or sitting with others, the smallest movement of her heart can become prayer.

His counsel makes room for the life she is actually living. She does not have to resolve her distress before seeking God's company. Her sorrow itself gives her something to bring, and a passing moment of gratitude offers another way to speak.

We may discover that prayer remains possible in forms we had overlooked. A silent request while washing our hands, a word of thanks at the table, or an inward acknowledgment of loneliness can open the conversation. God knows the strength we have today, and we can offer what is within reach without waiting to become more capable of offering it.

**Practice**

Choose one familiar moment today, such as drinking water, sitting down for a meal, or waiting for something to begin. Use that moment for a brief, honest remembrance of God. Ask for help, give thanks, or name something that hurts.

Let your words remain simple enough to fit the moment. If you cannot find words, pause with the intention of being present to God. Then continue with your day. Should another opportunity arise naturally, receive it without trying to establish a perfect rhythm. Allow each small prayer to stand on its own as an expression of relationship.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *You know my heart.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Receive this little prayer.*
