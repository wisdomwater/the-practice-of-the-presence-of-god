## Day 40 - Returning Without a Struggle

We sit down to pray and soon discover that we are somewhere else entirely. A conversation is unfolding in our imagination, tomorrow's responsibilities are arranging themselves, or an old embarrassment has returned. Once we notice, irritation can quickly follow, and we begin struggling with ourselves over our inability to remain attentive.

Brother Lawrence observes that this agitation makes recollection harder. The mind already has something drawing it away; our distress adds another concern for it to pursue. He recommends bringing attention back peacefully, with a willingness that can remain steady without becoming harsh.

This gives us something practical to do at the moment of discovery. We can acknowledge where our thoughts have gone, then return to the prayer or the quiet awareness with which we began. There is no need to retrace every distraction or determine how much time we have lost.

The return may be needed many times, especially when habits of distraction are well established. Brother Lawrence asks for perseverance while counseling tranquility, holding patience and intention together. We can practice both through a small movement of attention, learning to approach our wandering minds with enough kindness that prayer remains a place we are willing to enter again.

**Practice**

Spend three quiet minutes with a short prayer, such as “God, I am here with You.” Say it slowly, then allow a little silence. Whenever you notice that your thoughts have wandered, acknowledge the distraction briefly and return to the prayer.

Pay particular attention to your response when you discover the wandering. If irritation appears, let that feeling be acknowledged too. You do not have to become perfectly calm before returning. Simply soften whatever you can and begin again. At the end, offer thanks for the opportunity to practice, without counting distractions or judging the quality of your attention.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Receive my wandering heart.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I return to You gently.*
