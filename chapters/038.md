## Day 38 - Courage in God's Company

Before doing something difficult, we may wait for fear to loosen its hold. We hope to become composed enough to speak honestly, make a necessary decision, or face an uncertain outcome. Yet sometimes the moment arrives while our hands are still unsteady and our confidence remains incomplete.

Brother Lawrence writes about a young soldier exposed to danger. He encourages brief inward prayers, even while marching with a sword in hand, believing that remembrance of God can strengthen courage. Divine companionship belongs within the demanding moment, where attention and responsible action are urgently needed.

Our circumstances may be very different, but we can recognize the need for courage that accompanies us into difficulty. A short prayer can help us remember what we hope to serve and whom our choices affect. We may still feel afraid while becoming more available to wisdom, restraint, or a truthful response.

Courage takes different forms according to what love requires. We may need to speak, listen, seek assistance, or step away from harm. As we turn toward God, we can ask for enough steadiness to recognize the next faithful action and undertake it with care, even before our feelings have fully settled.

**Practice**

Choose one approaching situation in which you expect to need courage. Consider what a wise response might involve, including any preparation or support that would help. Name the quality you hope to bring, such as honesty, patience, or restraint.

Before entering the situation, offer a brief prayer: “God, help me act with courage and care.” Keep your attention on what is actually happening rather than trying to monitor how brave you feel. If possible, pause before responding. Afterward, acknowledge whatever effort you made and ask what you learned. Allow the experience to inform your next step without becoming a judgment of your strength.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *You accompany me.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Give me courage to act.*
