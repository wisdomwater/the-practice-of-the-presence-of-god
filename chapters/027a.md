## Fifth Letter — A Place of Prayer Within the Heart

*To Madame ——*  
*Undated*

Madame,

I feel deeply for you in your troubles. If you can entrust the management of your affairs to the gentleman and lady you mentioned, it would be of the greatest benefit to spend the remainder of your life giving yourself to the worship of God.

He asks no great burden of us: a little remembrance of Him from time to time, a brief act of adoration. Sometimes you can ask for His grace, sometimes offer Him your sorrows, and sometimes thank Him for the kindness He has already shown you and continues to show you amid your difficulties. He invites you to find comfort in His company as often as you can.

Lift your heart toward Him even while eating or spending time with others. The smallest remembrance will always be welcome to Him. You need not call loudly to make yourself heard, for He is nearer to us than we think.

We do not need to remain continually in church to be with God. We can make a little sanctuary within our hearts, withdrawing there from time to time for a gentle, humble, loving conversation with Him. Everyone can enjoy this closeness, though some will be able to give it more attention than others. God knows what each of us can do.

Let us begin, then. Perhaps He is waiting only for a wholehearted resolution on our part, so let us take courage. We have so little time left to live: you are nearly sixty-four, and I am almost eighty. Let us live and die with God.

While we remain with Him, even suffering will become sweet and bearable; without Him, the greatest pleasures will become bitter anguish. May He be blessed in everything. Amen.

Gradually accustom yourself to worshipping Him with all your strength, asking for His grace and offering Him your heart throughout the business of the day. Return to Him from time to time, and even at every moment if you can. Avoid becoming anxiously bound to fixed rules or particular forms of devotion. Proceed with faith in God, with love, and with humility.

Please assure the gentleman, the lady, and the young lady of my poor prayers and my willingness to serve them. I remain especially yours in our Lord.

Your brother.
