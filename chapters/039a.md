## Ninth Letter — Returning the Wandering Mind

*Undated*

When we begin a life of prayer without learning to govern our attention, the mind can acquire habits of wandering and distraction that become difficult to overcome. These habits often carry us toward earthly concerns even against our wishes. I believe one remedy is to confess our faults and humble ourselves before God.

I would not advise you to fill your prayer with many words. Lengthy reflections and elaborate expressions can themselves give the mind opportunities to wander. Instead, remain before God like a poor beggar at a rich person's gate, unable to speak or move, with nothing to offer except your need. Let your chief concern be to keep your attention in the Lord's presence.

If your mind wanders and draws away from Him, do not become greatly distressed. Anxiety and agitation tend to scatter attention further rather than gather it again. Gently exercise your will to bring your mind back in peace. If you persevere wholeheartedly, God will have compassion upon you.

One way to make this return easier during prayer is to attend to where your mind goes throughout the rest of the day. Keep bringing it into God's presence rather than allowing it to wander unchecked for long periods. As you grow accustomed to remembering Him frequently, you will find it easier to remain inwardly quiet during prayer—or at least to return when your attention strays.

In my other letters, I have already described at length the benefits we can receive through this practice of God's presence. Let us undertake it earnestly and pray for one another. Please remember me in your prayers, and ask the sister and the Reverend Mother to remember me also.

Yours in our Lord.
