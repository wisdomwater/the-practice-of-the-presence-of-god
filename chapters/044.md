## Day 44 - Letting Mercy Receive Our Regret

Looking back can reveal a painful distance between what we have received and how we have lived. We remember kindness we took for granted, opportunities we neglected, or promises that quietly faded. Gratitude and regret can arise together, leaving us uncertain how to respond to the years behind us.

After more than forty years in religious life, Brother Lawrence still questions how faithfully he has loved and served God. His letter expresses real shame over gifts poorly used. Yet he directs his next movement toward the Father of mercies, whom he describes as always ready to receive him with open arms.

His sorrow leads him toward relationship and a renewed willingness to respond. We can consider a similar movement when we face our own regrets. The past may call for apology, grief, or practical repair, while mercy gives us somewhere to bring what we cannot undo.

We do not have to resolve every feeling before accepting that welcome. We can confess what is true, ask for help, and allow ourselves to become available to love again. Within that return, gratitude may begin to nourish responsibility, helping us make better use of the care and opportunities still being offered today.

**Practice**

Recall one kindness or opportunity you believe you have received poorly. Choose something you can reflect upon without becoming overwhelmed. Give thanks for the gift, then speak honestly to God about your regret.

Ask whether a response remains possible. You might thank someone, renew a neglected commitment, or acknowledge a hurt. If action is available, take a modest step toward it. If the circumstances have passed, allow yourself to grieve their loss. End by imagining the open arms Brother Lawrence describes. Rest briefly with the possibility that mercy can receive both your sorrow and your unfinished desire to change.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Father of mercies, receive me.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Help me answer Your love.*
