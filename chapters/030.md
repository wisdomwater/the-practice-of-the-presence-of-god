## Day 30 - Letting Others Carry Their Part

Responsibility can become so familiar that we scarcely notice how much we are carrying. Even when others offer help, we may continue overseeing every detail, concerned that something will be missed. Gradually, our days fill with matters we feel unable to release, leaving little room to receive care ourselves.

Brother Lawrence begins his fifth letter with practical counsel: if his correspondent can entrust her affairs to others, she should do so. His encouragement toward prayer includes the possibility of setting down responsibilities. Within her troubled circumstances, accepting help could create space to attend more freely to God.

We may recognize how difficult such trust can be. Letting someone carry a task involves accepting their judgment, their methods, and the possibility that things will unfold differently from our preference. It also acknowledges that our strength has limits and that other people have gifts we need.

Where help is available, receiving it can become part of our spiritual life. We can discern what remains ours to do and what can be shared or entrusted to another. The space that opens may allow us to rest, pray, or become more present to a relationship that has been receiving only our leftover attention.

**Practice**

Consider one responsibility that has been weighing on you. Ask whether it truly requires your continued attention or whether someone else could reasonably carry part of it. If help is available, make one clear, modest request.

Agree on what is needed, then allow the other person room to act without unnecessary supervision. Use some of the space you receive for quiet, rest, or an unhurried conversation with God. If no one can help with the task itself, consider asking someone simply to listen. Let receiving support become an honest acknowledgment that you also need care.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Help me receive care.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I entrust what I can.*
