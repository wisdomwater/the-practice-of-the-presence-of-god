## Day 3 - The Gentle Art of Returning

There is something humbling about discovering how little control we have over our own attention. We may sit quietly with the intention of praying, only to find ourselves thinking about tomorrow's responsibilities, an unfinished conversation, or something we wish we had done differently. Even when we genuinely desire to remain attentive to God, our minds seem determined to wander in countless directions. After enough distractions, we may grow frustrated with ourselves, wondering whether we possess the discipline necessary for a meaningful spiritual life.

Brother Lawrence understood this struggle, having spent years learning to cultivate an awareness of God's presence. His practice did not depend upon maintaining perfect concentration or achieving an uninterrupted state of spiritual awareness. When he noticed that his attention had wandered, he simply turned his heart toward God again, trusting that divine companionship had remained available throughout his distraction. Over time, these repeated movements of attention became increasingly natural, much like the way we gradually become familiar with the presence of someone we deeply love.

We might consider how differently we approach a wandering mind when we understand prayer as a relationship rather than a measure of personal achievement. Imagine a child who becomes distracted while spending time with a loving parent, wandering off to explore something interesting before returning to share a new discovery. The parent does not need to interpret every moment of distraction as a rejection of their relationship. In a similar way, we might begin to understand our return to God as an expression of trust rather than an attempt to repair a relationship that has been damaged by our imperfect attention.

This gentle practice of returning can gradually reshape the way we relate to our own limitations. Rather than treating distraction as evidence of spiritual failure, we can receive each moment of renewed awareness as another opportunity for communion. Some days we may remember God's presence frequently, while other days may pass almost entirely within the demands of ordinary life. Yet even then, the invitation remains open, and we can begin again without needing to account for every moment we have been away.

**Practice**

Throughout the day, notice when your mind becomes absorbed in worries, distractions, or the endless stream of responsibilities. When you become aware of this, take a gentle breath and turn your attention toward God, even if only for a moment.

There is no need to interrupt your responsibilities or evaluate how long you have remained attentive. Allow the simple act of returning to be enough.

If you forget this practice entirely until the evening, that moment of remembering can become your opportunity to begin again. Receive it with kindness, trusting that God's presence has not depended upon your ability to remember.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *You welcome me again.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I return to Your love.*
