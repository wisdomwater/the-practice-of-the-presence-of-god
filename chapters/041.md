## Day 41 - When We Have Few Words

There are moments when words seem unable to carry what we need to express. We begin a prayer, revise it inwardly, and wonder whether we have explained ourselves clearly enough. The effort to speak properly can occupy the very attention we hoped to offer God.

Brother Lawrence recommends prayer with fewer words. He gives his reader a stark image: a poor beggar at a wealthy person's gate, unable to speak or move. The person's need is present before anything can be explained, and the hope of receiving help rests upon another's generosity.

We may find this image uncomfortable, especially if we are accustomed to approaching relationships through usefulness or competence. Yet it invites us to consider whether we can remain before God when we have little to contribute. Our need, honestly acknowledged, can give prayer its shape.

Words remain a gift, helping us name sorrow, gratitude, and desire. There are also times when we can let them fall quiet and trust that we are known. We can sit with the circumstances we cannot resolve, offering our presence and allowing ourselves to receive care without first producing an explanation worthy of the One who hears us.

**Practice**

Choose one need you would like to bring before God. Name it in a single sentence, using whatever words come naturally. Then sit quietly for a few moments without expanding the explanation.

If more words arise, notice whether they help you speak honestly or whether you are trying to make your need more deserving of attention. Gently return to your simple request when helpful. You might hold your hands open as an expression of willingness to receive. End with a brief word of trust, then consider whether there is a practical form of help you can seek or accept today.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *You already know my need.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I open myself to care.*
