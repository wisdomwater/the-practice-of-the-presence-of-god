## Day 54 - The Humility That Listens

When someone we care about is suffering, we may search our own experience for something that will help. We remember what sustained us and hope it might sustain them too. Yet the comfort we offer can become difficult to receive if we assume that their inward life should resemble our own.

Brother Lawrence writes from a place of abundant joy to a woman enduring illness. He wishes he could share her suffering, but acknowledges how weak he would be without God's help. His admission reveals the limits beneath his confidence: he cannot presume that he could easily bear what she is carrying.

This is a valuable place from which to accompany another person. We may have encouragement to offer while still needing to listen carefully to an experience we have not lived. What brings us peace may not bring them immediate relief, and their continuing distress deserves patient attention.

Humility allows our care to become more particular. We can ask what would help, accept an answer we did not anticipate, and offer something within our actual capacity. As we entrust the person to God, we can remain available without requiring their suffering to confirm our understanding of faith or their gratitude to reassure us that we have helped.

**Practice**

Bring to mind someone who is carrying a difficulty you have not experienced in the same way. Notice any explanation or advice you feel eager to offer. Hold it lightly while considering what you still do not know about their situation.

If appropriate, ask how they are doing and listen without redirecting the conversation toward your own experience. You might ask, “What would feel supportive right now?” Offer one specific form of care that you can realistically sustain. Later, pray for them without prescribing what their inward response should be. Let your care make room for the person as they are today.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Teach me to listen.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Let my care be humble.*
