## Day 27 - Staying Through the Resistance

Some days we approach prayer with very little desire to be there. Our thoughts feel restless, the minutes pass slowly, and almost anything else seems more useful. We may conclude that we should wait until we are more receptive, though the right mood can remain absent for a long time.

Brother Lawrence acknowledges this resistance directly. He warns that the practice may initially feel like wasted time and urges his reader to persevere. Yet the same letter counsels a peaceful return whenever attention wanders, inviting a steadiness that can endure difficulty without becoming agitated by it.

His language about overcoming reluctance is forceful, and we need wisdom in applying it. There are occasions when we need rest or a different approach, as well as occasions when remaining briefly with discomfort helps us grow. We can learn to distinguish these through honest attention to our circumstances and the effects of our practice.

Perhaps perseverance today means keeping one small appointment with God while allowing it to feel ordinary. We need not produce a moving prayer or explain why we are distracted. We can offer the willingness we have, trusting that a relationship can deepen through quiet acts whose significance becomes clear only over time.

**Practice**

Set aside a brief, realistic period for prayer today, perhaps three minutes. Begin by telling God how willing or unwilling you feel. Choose a simple way to remain present: a familiar prayer, quiet attention, or a few words spoken honestly.

When you feel the impulse to stop, notice it before deciding what to do. If you are simply restless, gently stay for another breath or two. If you recognize genuine exhaustion, let your prayer become a moment of rest. Finish without grading the experience. Give thanks for whatever willingness was available, and allow your next return to begin from there.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Sustain my small willingness.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Help me stay with You.*
