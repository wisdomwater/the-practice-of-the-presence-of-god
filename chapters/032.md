## Day 32 - The King Who Makes Room

When we have hurt someone or betrayed our own convictions, we may approach the truth cautiously. We prepare an explanation, anticipate disappointment, and wonder what our admission will cost. Even when forgiveness is offered, we can remain inwardly distant, unsure whether we are welcome to draw near again.

Brother Lawrence imagines himself standing before his King, confessing serious offenses and surrendering to His judgment. The scene carries the severity of his own self-understanding. Yet the King's response overflows with tenderness: He embraces him, seats him at the table, serves him with His own hands, and opens His treasures to him.

The detail of being served is especially striking. Brother Lawrence brings his sorrow before God and discovers a generosity he could never have required or earned. Within his prayer, confession becomes an encounter in which mercy restores the possibility of companionship.

We can let this image question the distance we continue to maintain after acknowledging a wrong. There may be amends to make and patterns that need patient attention. Alongside that work, we can begin receiving the welcome we have been afraid to trust, allowing God's kindness to nourish the very changes we long to make.

**Practice**

Read slowly through the image of the merciful King: the embrace, the place at the table, the meal served by His own hands. Notice which detail draws your attention and which feels difficult to receive.

Bring one regret into prayer, naming it plainly without adding a judgment about your entire worth. Ask whether an apology or practical act of repair is needed. Then spend a moment with the possibility of being welcomed while that work remains unfinished. You do not have to manufacture a feeling of forgiveness. Simply allow the image to accompany you as you take your next faithful step.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *You make room for me.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Help me receive Your mercy.*
