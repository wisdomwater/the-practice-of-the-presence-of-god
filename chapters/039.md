## Day 39 - Keeping the Heart Awake

Under pressure, our attention can narrow until completing the task seems to matter more than anything else. We speak sharply, overlook someone's hesitation, or accept a shortcut we would question in calmer circumstances. Only afterward do we recognize how easily urgency displaced the values we intended to live by.

Brother Lawrence's concern for the young soldier extends beyond physical danger. He also worries about the dangers to his spiritual life and encourages a quiet habit of remembering God. The prayer he recommends can accompany action, sustaining an inward relationship where circumstances might otherwise absorb the whole person.

We might receive this counsel as an invitation to keep our hearts awake within demanding environments. Remembering God can recall us to the dignity of the person before us and the kind of response we hope to offer. A brief inward pause may create room to notice what haste has concealed.

This awareness grows through small, repeated choices. We learn to recognize when fatigue makes us careless or when a desire to succeed begins to excuse unnecessary harm. Bringing those discoveries into prayer, we can seek help, repair what we can, and allow our responsibilities to become places where love continues shaping our conduct.

**Practice**

Notice one setting where pressure makes it harder to act according to your values. Identify an early sign that your attention is narrowing: a hurried tone, clenched muscles, impatience, or the thought that you must win.

When that sign appears today, use it as a gentle invitation to remember God. Ask, “What needs care here?” Look for one concrete response, such as lowering your voice, checking an assumption, or giving someone room to finish speaking. If you notice only afterward, consider whether repair is possible. Let the remembrance help you remain accountable and available to love within the work itself.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Keep my heart awake.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Let love guide my actions.*
