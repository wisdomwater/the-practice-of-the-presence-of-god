## Second Letter — The Treasure Within

*To the Reverend Mother*  
*June 1, 1682*

My Reverend Mother,

I am taking this opportunity to share the thoughts of a brother in our community concerning the wonderful effects of God's presence and the continual help he receives through it. May both of us benefit from what he has discovered.

For more than forty years in religious life, his constant concern has been to remain with God. He has sought to do nothing, say nothing, and think nothing that might displease Him. His sole intention has been to act for the love of God, who deserves infinitely more love than we can offer.

He has now become so accustomed to this divine presence that he receives assistance from it in every circumstance. For more than thirty years, his soul has been filled with an almost continual joy. Sometimes this joy becomes so overwhelming that he must deliberately restrain its outward expression.

When his attention strays somewhat from God, as often happens during demanding outward work, God soon makes His presence felt within him and draws him back. He responds faithfully to these inward invitations, sometimes by lifting his heart toward God, sometimes with a gentle and loving inward glance, and sometimes with a few words that arise naturally from affection. He might pray, “My God, see me here, entirely Yours,” or, “Lord, shape me according to Your heart.”

Then it seems to him—and this is how he experiences it—that the God of love, content with these few words, settles again into the deepest center of his soul. Such experiences have given him an unwavering assurance that God dwells within him. Whatever may happen, he finds himself unable to doubt that presence.

You can imagine the contentment of someone who continually carries such a treasure within. He no longer searches anxiously for what he fears may be beyond his reach. The treasure lies open before him, freely available for him to receive.

He often laments how little we recognize of what God offers and how readily we content ourselves with so little. God's treasure, he says, is like an infinite ocean, yet we are satisfied with a small wave of feeling that passes in a moment. In our blindness, we hinder God and obstruct the flow of His grace. When He finds a soul filled with living faith, however, He pours His gifts into it abundantly.

This outpouring resembles a torrent that has been held back from its natural course. Once an opening appears, the accumulated waters rush through with tremendous force. Yet we often hold back this torrent ourselves because we place so little value upon what God desires to give. Let us hinder it no longer, but turn inward and remove the barrier that obstructs its passage.

Let us make full use of this time of grace and recover what we can of the time already lost. Perhaps little remains to us, for death follows close behind. We must prepare ourselves well: we die only once, and failure at that final passage cannot be undone.

Again, I urge us to turn inward. Time presses upon us, and our souls are at stake; we cannot afford to delay. I believe you have already taken such earnest measures that you will not be caught unprepared, and I commend you for attending to this one necessary thing. Nevertheless, we must persevere, for in the spiritual life, to cease advancing is to begin falling back.

Those whose spirits are moved by the breath of the Holy Spirit continue forward even while they sleep. If the little boat of our soul is still being tossed by winds and storms, let us awaken the Lord who rests within it. He will soon calm the sea.

I have ventured to share these thoughts so that you may consider them alongside your own. Should your fervor have cooled even slightly—which God forbid, for that would indeed be a serious loss—they may help kindle it again. Let us both renew the love and earnestness of our beginnings.

May we benefit from the example and reflections of this brother, who is little known to the world but known to God and cherished by Him beyond telling. I will pray for you. Please pray earnestly for me in return.

Yours in our Lord.
