## Tenth Letter — Growing at the Pace of Grace

*To the same correspondent*  
*March 28, 1689*

The enclosed letter is my reply to the one I received from our good sister. Please deliver it to her. She seems full of good intentions, but she wants to move faster than grace. We do not become holy all at once.

I entrust her to your care. We ought to help one another through our counsel, and still more through the example of our lives. Please let me know how she is doing from time to time, particularly whether she remains wholehearted in her devotion and faithful in obedience.

Let us remember often that our great purpose in this life is to please God. Everything else may prove to be little more than foolishness and vanity. You and I have spent more than forty years in religious life. Have we used those years to love and serve the God who mercifully called us into this life for that very purpose?

I am filled with shame and dismay when I consider the great kindness God has shown me and continues to show me without ceasing. Beside His generosity, I see how poorly I have used His gifts and how little progress I have made toward holiness. Yet His mercy still grants us a little time. Let us use it to begin again in earnest and recover what we can of the years we have lost.

Let us return with wholehearted confidence to this Father of mercies, who is always ready to receive us into His loving arms. For His love, let us freely and generously relinquish everything that does not belong to Him. He deserves infinitely more than we can give. Let us remember Him continually and place all our confidence in Him.

I do not doubt that we will soon experience the fruits of this trust through an abundance of grace. With His grace, we can do all things; without it, we can do nothing but sin. The dangers of life are too numerous for us to navigate without God's active and continual assistance. Let us therefore keep asking Him for the help we need.

But how can we pray to Him without being with Him? And how can we remain with Him unless we remember Him often? Such remembrance grows through a holy habit of attention that we must gradually cultivate.

You may tell me that I am always repeating the same thing. It is true. This is the best and simplest way I know, and because it is the way I follow myself, I recommend it to everyone.

We must come to know before we can love. To know God, we must turn our thoughts toward Him often. As love grows, it draws our attention back to Him all the more readily, for our hearts remain with what we treasure.

Consider this often, and give it your careful attention.

Yours in our Lord.
