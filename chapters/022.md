## Day 22 - Carried by One Another

When we stand before a significant commitment, we may discover how much we need other people. A decision that seemed clear in private becomes more vulnerable as the moment approaches. We want someone to remember us, to hope with us, and to help us remain faithful when our own confidence wavers.

Brother Lawrence begins his third letter with the request of a sister preparing to make her religious vows. She has asked for the community's prayers, and he urges the Reverend Mother to honor her trust. Before offering any teaching about inward attention, he attends to this particular person and the support she needs.

Her request reminds us that giving ourselves to God can include allowing others to accompany us. We may be willing to encourage someone else while finding it difficult to admit that we need encouragement ourselves. Asking for prayer makes our dependence visible and gives another person an opportunity to express love.

Brother Lawrence closes the letter by promising his prayers and requesting theirs. Spiritual companionship moves in both directions, with each person having something to offer and something to receive. Within that shared care, our commitments can become less lonely, sustained by a love that reaches us through one another.

**Practice**

Bring to mind someone approaching a meaningful commitment or transition. It might involve marriage, caregiving, a new responsibility, or a difficult decision. Pray for that person in specific terms, asking that love and wisdom shape what lies ahead.

If appropriate, let them know you are remembering them, without requiring an explanation or response. Then consider one place where you would welcome support yourself. You might ask someone you trust to pray for you or simply tell them what you are carrying. Allow the exchange to remain modest and sincere, making room for both your generosity and your need.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Hold us in Your love.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *Teach us to receive care.*
