## Day 20 - The Gentle Invitation

Sometimes we notice how long we have been moving through the day without really attending to it. Our minds have been occupied with unfinished work, imagined conversations, and whatever appeared on the screen. Then something interrupts the momentum, and for a moment we become available to where we are.

Brother Lawrence described God drawing his attention back during demanding work. He responded with an inward movement of affection, a loving glance, or a few simple words. The renewed awareness arrived as an invitation he could answer within the activity already before him.

This offers another way to understand the moment when we remember God. We often treat it as evidence of how thoroughly we had forgotten, immediately turning our attention toward the lapse. Yet the remembrance itself can be received with gratitude, giving us a place to begin without first examining the hours that preceded it.

We need not determine whether every inward prompting has come directly from God. We can respond to the opportunity it presents with a brief prayer and a more attentive return to our surroundings. A moment of recollection may help us hear someone more fully, notice our own weariness, or approach the next responsibility with a little more love.

**Practice**

When you remember God today, pause before assessing how well you have maintained your attention. Receive the remembrance as an opening. Offer a few words that fit the moment: “I am here,” “Help me listen,” or, “Receive what I am doing.”

Then notice one particular thing before you: a person's expression, the task in your hands, or the place where your body is tense. Let your prayer lead you into fuller attention to that reality. If you forget again, allow the next remembrance to offer another beginning. There is no need to keep count or reconstruct how long you were distracted.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *You draw me near.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I answer with love.*
