## First Letter — Learning to Belong Wholly to God

*To the Reverend Mother*  
*Undated*

My Reverend Mother,

You have asked so earnestly that I explain how I came to live with this habitual awareness of God's presence, which our Lord in His mercy has granted me. I have been very reluctant to speak of it, and I yield to your repeated requests only on the condition that you show this letter to no one. If I thought you would allow others to read it, even my sincere desire for your spiritual growth would not persuade me to write.

The account I can offer is simple. I found many books describing different ways of approaching God and recommending various spiritual practices. Their variety seemed more likely to confuse me than to help me find what I desired, which was simply to belong wholly to God. I therefore resolved to give myself entirely to Him, offering everything I was in response to the One who is everything.

Having surrendered myself to God, and desiring to make whatever amends I could for my sins, I renounced for His love everything that did not belong to Him. I began to live as though God and I were alone together in the world. Sometimes I placed myself before Him as a guilty person at the feet of a judge. At other times, I turned toward Him within my heart as my Father and my God.

I worshipped Him as often as I could, keeping my attention in His holy presence and bringing it back whenever I noticed that it had wandered. This practice cost me considerable effort, and I encountered many difficulties. Nevertheless, I continued, taking care not to become anxious or distressed when my attention strayed involuntarily. Whenever I discovered that I had wandered, I returned to Him.

I made this my concern throughout the day, just as much as during the hours appointed for prayer. At every hour and every moment, even in the busiest part of my work, I sought to turn away whatever interrupted my remembrance of God. This has been my ordinary practice ever since I entered religious life. Although I have followed it very imperfectly, I have received great help through it.

I know that every benefit must be attributed entirely to God's mercy and goodness. We can do nothing without Him, and I believe myself less capable than anyone. Yet when we faithfully remain in His presence and keep Him before us, we become less willing to offend Him or knowingly do anything that displeases Him. At the same time, a holy freedom begins to grow within us, together with what I can only describe as a familiarity with God.

Within that familiarity, we learn to ask freely for the grace we need, and we experience His help. Through frequently renewing these acts of attention and love, they gradually become habitual. The awareness of God's presence becomes, as it were, natural to us.

Please join me in thanking Him for His great goodness toward me. I can never sufficiently marvel at the many favors He has shown to so miserable a sinner as I am. May all things praise Him.

Amen.

Yours in our Lord.
