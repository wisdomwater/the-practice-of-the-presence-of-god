## Day 53 - As a Child Asks for Help

When we have been struggling for a long time, asking for help can become strangely difficult. We may feel that we have already asked too often or that we should have learned to bear things more gracefully. Our need remains, while an inward pressure urges us to become less troublesome.

Brother Lawrence encourages his correspondent to ask for grace as a child turns toward a good father. He places this invitation at the height of her suffering, when composure and carefully chosen words may be least available. Her weakness gives her a reason to approach rather than a reason to hold back.

The image rests upon the goodness of the one receiving the request. A child who trusts a caregiver can name pain before understanding it and seek comfort before knowing what would help. We can explore that same simplicity in prayer, bringing the need we have instead of waiting for a more acceptable one.

Some of us will find this image easier to receive than others. Where memories of care are complicated, trust may need time and patient companionship. We can begin with one plain request, allowing our dependence to be acknowledged and leaving room for help to reach us through both prayer and the people around us.

**Practice**

Notice one need you have hesitated to express because it feels repetitive, inconvenient, or difficult to explain. Bring it into prayer in a single, direct sentence. You might say, “I need comfort,” “Help me bear this,” or, “I do not know what I need.”

Pause without adding an apology for asking. If the image of a good parent helps, imagine your request being received with patient attention. If it does not, simply remain with the possibility of kindness. Consider whether someone could offer practical or emotional support today. Make a clear request if you can, allowing your need to be heard.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *Receive me with kindness.*
>
> **Breathing out:** *I ask for what I need.*
