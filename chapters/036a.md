## Eighth Letter — Remembering God in Times of Danger

*To Madame ——*  
*October 12, 1688*

Madame,

Our God is infinitely gracious and knows everything we need. I had long thought that He would allow you to be brought to the end of your resources. He will come to your aid in His own time, when you least expect Him. Place your hope in Him more firmly than ever.

Join me in thanking Him for the kindness He shows you, especially for the courage and patience He gives you amid your afflictions. I regard these gifts as clear signs of His care for you. Take comfort in His company, then, and give thanks for everything.

I also admire the strength and bravery of Monsieur de ——. God has given him a good nature and a willing heart, although some attachment to the world remains in him, along with much of the impetuousness of youth. I hope the affliction God has sent him will serve as a healing medicine and lead him to examine his life. It offers a fitting opportunity for him to place his whole confidence in the One who accompanies him everywhere.

Encourage him to remember God as often as he can, especially when danger is greatest. A brief lifting of the heart, a moment of remembrance, or a single inward act of worship is enough. Even while marching with a sword in his hand, he can offer these little prayers. However short they may be, they are very pleasing to God and can strengthen a soldier’s courage.

Let him gradually become accustomed to this small but holy practice. No one else needs to notice it, and these brief acts of inward worship can be repeated often throughout the day. Please encourage him to turn toward God in this way whenever he can. I consider it especially fitting and necessary for a soldier, whose life is exposed daily to danger and whose salvation is often endangered as well.

I hope that God will help him and all your family. Please convey to them my willingness to serve them. I remain theirs, and especially yours.

Yours in our Lord.
