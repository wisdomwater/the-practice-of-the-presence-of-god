## Day 6 - Faithfulness in the Dry Seasons

There are times when prayer feels natural and comforting, when gratitude rises easily and we experience a quiet sense of connection with God. Yet there are other seasons when the same practices seem strangely empty. We may sit in silence and encounter only our restless thoughts, or speak familiar words of prayer without sensing any response. If we have come to associate divine presence with particular feelings of peace or consolation, these experiences can leave us wondering whether something has gone wrong.

Brother Lawrence spoke openly about periods of spiritual dryness, when prayer becomes wearisome and the soul experiences little sense of comfort. He believed these seasons offered opportunities for faithfulness, inviting us to surrender ourselves to God even when our emotions provided little encouragement. For him, love was something deeper than the changing sensations that accompanied spiritual practice. A sincere act of trust during a difficult season could carry great significance precisely because it arose without the reassurance of immediate consolation.

This understanding asks us to consider the nature of love in our other relationships. A friendship does not cease to exist when conversation becomes difficult, nor does devotion disappear when exhaustion prevents us from feeling especially affectionate. Relationships gradually deepen through ordinary acts of presence, patience, and care, including those moments when our emotions offer little reward.

Perhaps our relationship with God can mature in a similar way. We need not manufacture spiritual feelings or interpret their absence as evidence of abandonment. Instead, we can continue offering whatever attention, honesty, and willingness we possess, trusting that communion may be unfolding in ways we cannot presently perceive. Even an uncertain prayer can become an expression of love when we bring ourselves to God as we are.

**Practice**

Set aside a few quiet moments today to be present with God without seeking a particular emotional experience. You might sit comfortably, close your eyes, or simply become aware of your breathing.

Notice whatever arises within you, whether peace, restlessness, gratitude, frustration, or uncertainty. Rather than trying to change these feelings, allow them to be part of an honest encounter with God.

If prayer feels difficult, you might quietly acknowledge, "I am here, even when I do not know what to say." Let this simple willingness be enough for today, trusting that your relationship with God does not depend upon the intensity of your feelings.

**Breath Prayer**

> **Breathing in:** *You remain with me.*
> **Breathing out:** *I offer You my trust.*
