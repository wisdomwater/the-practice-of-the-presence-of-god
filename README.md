# The Practice of the Presence of God

### A 60-Day Journey into Divine Awareness, Simplicity, and Love

*A contemporary devotional adaptation of Brother Lawrence’s conversations and letters, by Michael Collins.*

## About the Book

Brother Lawrence’s approach to prayer grew within the ordinary responsibilities of a seventeenth-century Carmelite monastery. In the kitchen and throughout his daily work, he cultivated a simple awareness of God’s presence through inward conversation, acts of love, and patient returns after distraction.

This book invites contemporary readers to explore that same possibility within their own lives. Work, rest, relationships, and uncertainty become occasions for communion as we learn to offer our attention and respond with care.

The book combines modern retellings of Brother Lawrence’s four recorded conversations and fifteen letters with sixty original devotional readings. Its central invitation is to recognize and receive God’s presence amid the circumstances we already inhabit.

## Structure

The retellings appear before their corresponding devotionals and are separate from the sixty numbered readings.

| Section | Source Material | Devotional Days |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | Three introductory reflections | 1–3 |
| Part I: Discovering the Presence | Conversations 1–4 | 4–15 |
| Part II: Learning the Practice | Letters 1–5 | 16–30 |
| Part III: Growing in Trust | Letters 6–10 | 31–45 |
| Part IV: Presence Through Suffering | Letters 11–15 | 46–60 |

A preface introduces the book’s purpose and approach. An epilogue sends readers into everyday life with an invitation to continue practicing.

## The Daily Readings

Each devotional includes:

- **Reflection:** A contemporary exploration of themes arising from the corresponding conversation or letter.
- **Practice:** An accessible invitation to carry the teaching into ordinary life.
- **Breath Prayer:** Two short phrases accompanying a natural breath, offering a simple way to remember the day’s theme.

Readers can follow the sixty-day sequence, linger over a particular entry, or revisit a practice as circumstances change.

## Themes

Throughout the book, the readings explore:

- Awareness of God within ordinary activities.
- Prayer as an ongoing relationship.
- Small acts offered with love.
- Grace, imperfection, and beginning again.
- Trust that includes responsible action and receiving help.
- Companionship amid suffering and uncertainty.
- Growing in humility, gratitude, and attentiveness.

The tone is contemplative, compassionate, and rooted in the Christian tradition, with room for questions and the complexity of lived experience.

## Approach to the Source

The retellings use contemporary English while seeking to preserve Brother Lawrence’s meaning, imagery, historical details, and difficult teachings. They are adaptations rather than a scholarly translation.

The devotionals are original reflections that engage his teaching through contemporary experience. They distinguish his historical perspective from the interpretations and applications offered to readers today.

Some letters contain severe language about sin, suffering, and self-denial, as well as historical advice about illness and medical remedies. These passages are retained for an honest encounter with the source. They should not be treated as medical guidance or as reasons to refuse treatment, practical assistance, or appropriate care.
